Weekly roundup, week of 29 August to 4 September 2026

Top ERP news this week

Sage expands AI across Sage X3, aimed squarely at manufacturers and distributors

4 September 2026

Sage announced a set of updates to Sage X3 that push AI further into day to day operations rather than treating it as a bolt on. Sage Copilot now surfaces AI generated insights across finance, inventory, sales and production to flag risks and opportunities, while keeping the final call with the human user. Alongside this, Sage introduced Sage X3 SaaS, a managed cloud version that takes infrastructure and update management off the customer's plate, a new partnership with Lynq for shop floor production visibility, and AI powered e-invoicing for French customers to meet local digital compliance rules. Rob Sinfield, SVP of ERP at Sage, framed it as decision support rather than autonomous decision making, which fits the wider industry pattern of keeping a human in the loop on regulated processes.

Cegid brings native AI to its mid-market ERPs, Primavera Evolution and PHC Evolution

18 August 2026 (surfaced this week, not previously covered)

Cegid has integrated a native AI layer called Cegid Pulse into Cegid Primavera Evolution and Cegid PHC Evolution, two ERPs widely used by small and mid sized businesses in Portugal and Spain. It works through two mechanisms: Smart Assistants, a conversational layer that answers contextual questions and supports decisions such as treasury forecasts, and Smart Actions, which proactively sends risk alerts, generates reports and executes routine tasks without a user prompting it. Practical uses cited include automated monthly financial closing with inconsistency detection, resume driven onboarding, and predictive stock management with automatic purchase order generation. Cegid is also among the early signatories of the EU's AI Pact, which it is using to frame the launch around ethical and controlled AI use rather than pure automation.

Sankhya launches an AI agent that audits and monitors the ERP itself, continuously

4 September 2026

Brazilian ERP vendor Sankhya announced Reploy Agent, embedded in its Sankhya OM platform, which runs around the clock inside production environments to catch security risks, configuration failures and performance issues caused by customizations, rather than waiting for a scheduled audit. It also watches external connections, third party API usage and data integrity, and it is included at no extra cost within standard pricing rather than sold as an add on. It follows Sankhya's earlier Deploy Agent and EIP Cognitivo releases, so this reads as the next step in a deliberate AI-first roadmap rather than a one-off feature. Worth watching as an example of AI being pointed at the ERP's own operational health, not just at end user workflows.

Notable trends or shifts worth watching

  • AI agents cutting ERP implementation time in half. McKinsey's ongoing analysis argues that AI agents can already cut the effort needed to implement an ERP system by at least 50 percent and roughly halve program duration, which would meaningfully change the economics of ERP rollouts for vendors and implementation partners alike. Originally published in May 2026, but still the most cited framing in recent coverage. McKinsey, The end of ERP as we know it
  • Consolidation pressure building in enterprise software. AlixPartners has forecast that AI driven disruption will push M&A activity in enterprise software up 30 to 40 percent year on year, toward roughly 600 billion dollars in 2026, as smaller and mid-market vendors struggle to keep pace with AI investment demands. Worth watching for how it plays out among mid-market ERP vendors specifically, not just the giants.
  • Buyers have the tools but not the integration. Distribution Strategy Group's 2026 State of Distributor Technology research found 55 percent of surveyed distributors have already invested in core systems, ERP, CRM, ecommerce and analytics, without fully integrating them. The stated next phase for these buyers is less about buying more software and more about making what they already own talk to each other, a useful data point against any pitch that leads with more modules.
  • Brazil's AI-in-ERP maturity gap. Coverage circulating in the Brazilian market this week cites Exame reporting that 84 percent of Brazilian companies use AI "in pieces," isolated initiatives without full integration into processes or strategy, even though 99 percent have started or plan to start an AI journey. Only 7.5 percent sit at an advanced digital maturity stage with dedicated AI budget and ROI tracking. It is a useful reminder that AI-in-ERP hype and AI-in-ERP maturity are two different curves, not just in Brazil.

Relevant to a mid-market distribution ERP vendor

  • Both headline items this week (Sage X3 and Cegid) are aimed at exactly Iptor's buyer profile: mid-market manufacturers and distributors, not large enterprise. Sage's shop floor visibility partnership with Lynq and its AI e-invoicing push are both areas where Iptor's own exception handling and compliance story could be compared directly.
  • The Distribution Strategy Group integration gap (55 percent invested but not integrated) is a live opening for positioning a platform like IUP as the thing that connects already purchased tools, rather than another system to bolt on.
  • Sankhya's Reploy Agent is a reminder that "AI in the ERP" does not have to mean customer facing copilots. Pointing AI inward, at auditing customizations and catching configuration drift before it causes an incident, is a different value story that could resonate with IUP customers running heavily customized DC1 environments.
